# AI Quotient: Orchestrate. Harness. Or be orchestrated.

**26.09.21 · Beto’s Take**

AI Quotient (AIQ) is how well you use AI, check its work, and lead others doing the same.

> Give AI clear jobs. Keep the work organized. Check what comes back. Or someone else will decide how your expertise fits into their process.

## You know the work. Become the conductor.

Your expertise and judgment are the advantage. AI helps with the parts; you judge the whole.

| Step | Human responsibility | AI’s role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Research | Define what is useful and relevant | Find useful material |
| Draft | Set the standard and shape the argument | Prepare a first version |
| Check | Challenge the result and correct it | Surface a result to review |

## Three ideas behind the thesis

### 01 · Your experience is the advantage

AI makes answers easier to get. You still need to know which questions matter, what good work looks like, and when an answer does not make sense.

### 02 · More agents do not automatically mean better work

Give each agent a distinct job. Judge the result after checking and corrections. What is fun, what is productive, and what is monetizable are different.

### 03 · Inside an organization, prove one useful improvement

Get permission. Try one small piece of work. Have someone else check it. Then discuss what the improvement changes for your role—not just your workload.

## I did not start with agents. I started with spreadsheets.

I turned spreadsheets into tools. Then came Linear, repeatable skills, local models in LM Studio, and now Orca to organize agent work. Each step meant learning—and rework.

My point: experience matters more when you can turn it into a way of working others can use.

## Where this goes

These are possibilities, not predictions:

- **Agents keep working between your check-ins.** More work continues while you are away. Someone must review what changed.
- **Agents work together across organizations.** Your agent may coordinate with theirs. Who is allowed to make a commitment?
- **Decisions move faster than human oversight.** The risk is that promises pile up before people can understand or stop them.

Trust, permission, money, and the physical world do not move at software speed.
